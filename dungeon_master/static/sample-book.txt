THE HOLLOW LANTERN
An original miniature setting for testing your book library.

THE CITY OF BRIARWELL

Briarwell sits where the Alder River disappears beneath a limestone ridge. Its houses climb the steep hillside, and rope bridges connect the rooftops above the crowded market. The western gate closes at sunset. Visitors arriving late can shelter at the Copper Kettle, an inn run by the retired ranger Mara Venn.

Mara knows that something has changed beneath the city. The well water tastes of iron, and a green light sometimes shines through the cracks in the cellar floor. She offers travelers twenty silver crowns to investigate the abandoned aqueduct. She also lends them a brass lantern that burns with a cold blue flame near enchanted stone.

THE RUINS BELOW

An old staircase behind the market descends to the aqueduct. Three flooded chambers lead to a sealed shrine. Blind cave salamanders nest in the first chamber. They hunt by vibration, so walking softly across the raised stone ledges avoids attracting them. The second chamber contains a broken mechanism that controls the floodgate. A silver gear hidden beneath the silt can repair it.

The shrine is guarded by a moss-covered stone sentinel. Its chest holds a hollow lantern containing the memory of an ancient guardian. The sentinel does not attack anyone who speaks the guardian's name, Orinth. That name is carved on the underside of the floodgate's control wheel. If awakened peacefully, the sentinel explains that a stolen amber seal is poisoning the city's water.

MAGIC AND TREASURES

The lantern of quiet stars sheds soft light in a thirty-foot circle. Once each night, its bearer can dim the flame to reveal footprints left by spirits during the previous hour. It cannot reveal living creatures or predict where a spirit will travel. Its light fades at sunrise and returns after an hour beneath the open night sky.

The amber seal belongs in a socket behind the shrine. Restoring it cleans the water and frees the sentinel from its long vigil. Taking the seal away causes the aqueduct to flood at the next full moon. A patient adventurer can learn this from the mural surrounding the shrine door, which depicts the river rising around a figure holding the seal.

FACES AND SECRETS

Edda Thorn, the city's archivist, secretly removed the seal to save her brother from a wasting illness. She believes it contains a healing spirit. She can be found at the observatory after dusk, studying the seal through a cracked telescope. Edda will surrender it if the travelers find another way to help her brother. The herbalist Pell grows moonwort in a rooftop garden; a tea made from its silver leaves can treat the illness.

The story can begin when Mara invites the travelers to share a table at the Copper Kettle. Rain rattles against the shutters. A child enters carrying a cup of green water, and a distant bell rings from somewhere beneath the floor.
